Question 3.1 Report the slope, its p-value, and the R-squared. Is ECV significantly associated with age in this model? What does the sign of the slope tell you biologically?

  • Slope = -249.14, p-value < 0.0001, R-squared: 0.750 or 75% of the variance. Yes, ECV is significantly associated with age. Biologically, this reflects the overall trend of increased brain size in the hominin lineage, from small-brained early hominins to the larger brains of later Homo.

Question 3.2: Compare R-squared between model1 and model2. Does adding grade improve fit? Interpret the C(grade)[T.pre.mod] coefficient what do its sign and size tell you about pre.mod specimens compared with archaic specimens of the same age?

  • Adding grade did improve the fit as the R-squared increased from 75% to 80%. As more parameters are introduced, the fit will improve. Adding grade also reduced the age slope from −294 to −226 cm3/Ma, meaning part of the age effect in model1 reflected which grades are present at different times. The coefficient of C(grade)[T.pre.mod] is 113.1 cm3. Because archaic is the reference grade and age is in the model, this is the difference in predicted ECV between a pre.mod specimen and an archaic specimen of the same age. The sign is positive and the size is about 113 cm3.

#| label: fig-ecv-fitted

pred = pd.DataFrame(
    {"age_mid_Ma": np.linspace(ecv["age_mid_Ma"].min(), ecv["age_mid_Ma"].max(), 200)}
)
pred["ecv_pred"] = model1.predict(pred)

(
    ggplot(
        ecv, aes(x="age_mid_Ma", y="ecv_median_cm3", color="grade", size="n_estimates")
    )
    + geom_point(alpha=0.6, size=2)
    + geom_line(
        aes(x="age_mid_Ma", y="ecv_pred"),
        data=pred,
        color="black",
        size=1,
        inherit_aes=False,
    )
    + scale_x_reverse()
    + labs(x="Age (Ma)", y="ECV (cm³)", color="Grade", title="Hominin ECV Through Time")
